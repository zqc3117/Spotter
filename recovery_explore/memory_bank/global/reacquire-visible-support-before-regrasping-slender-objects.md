**Why:** Both arms failed. In the treatment arm, an early attempt to reposition for a missed carrot grasp caused extreme overshoot and was undone. The policy subsequently moved the carrot outward, but it then visibly slipped as the fingers nearly closed together. Opening, lifting, retreating, and nudging did not recover the task; the intervention record never established a visible landing position. This supports testing a recovery tied to the object's new support surface, rather than continuing from its former grasp location.

**How to apply:** After a visible slip, first use a small clearance move to reveal where the object has come to rest. Require a visible, stationary object and its supporting surface before attempting a regrasp. If reachable, approach its exposed midsection with open fingers from a direction clear of the fixture lip, placing the fingers on opposite sides at the object's current height. Hand back for the policy to close once alignment is visible, then check that the object follows a short extraction before continuing transport. This is an untested alternative; if the landing remains hidden, do not infer a grasp target from the old pose.

**Falsify:** In a subsequent comparable slip, establish the support surface and visible finger alignment, then let the policy close. If the fingers still close empty or the object slips on the first short extraction despite that alignment, support-based reacquisition alone is insufficient; the proposed approach and handoff need revision.
