**Why:** Both control and treatment failed. Reopening and resuming the policy, lowering a previous missed grasp, and trying a hover followed by descent, controlled close, and lift did not establish a retained grasp. Changing wrist orientation also failed. These results suggest testing object-relative finger placement instead of another correction anchored to the missed hand pose; they do not establish the cause of failure.

**How to apply:** On the next similar failure, approach through the shelf opening with the fingers open and use the current view to place the produce's broad middle between the opposing finger pads. Check clearance for both fingers from the shelf and cabinet before descending further. Make the close explicitly once that placement is visible, then lift just enough to verify that the object moves with the hand. Hand transport back to the policy only after retention is visible. If the finger placement cannot be seen, obtain a clearer view before committing to the close.

**Falsify:** This alternative is unsupported if a clearly observed pinch around the broad middle, with both fingers clear of the fixture, still closes empty or loses the object during the verification lift. A retained lift followed by a transport drop would instead show that this proposal does not address the remaining transport failure.
