Watch for commanded displacement that grows chunk over chunk (the controller chasing a target it cannot reach) while travel stays under about a centimetre, fingers are shut on nothing, and the knob index does not move against the panel. That is a blocked hand, not a slow press. A hand that is really turning a knob gets steady commands, not escalating ones.

**Why:** A small back-off followed by a hand-back only frees the arm. The policy just failed from that pose. Once freed, it went somewhere new (below the knob row) rather than back onto the knob, and it spent the remaining steps there. The intervention got the arm moving again but never put the fingers on the knob, so the burner never changed.

**How to apply:** Before acting, name the target knob. Use a third-person row to see which burner is lit, then map it to its position in the knob row, and check that position in the wrist row. Then open the fingers, back off, and restage level with that knob, with the fingers open and straddling it. Close on the knob and rotate in small steps yourself, checking the index mark against the panel after each rotation. Hand back only after the index has visibly moved, or with the fingers seated on the knob, never from a pose that is merely clear of the obstruction. Treat two windows of escalating commands with near-zero travel and an unchanged index as enough to intervene. Do not wait a third window.

**Falsify:** If on a similar stall a restage onto the identified knob, followed by your own close and rotate, still leaves the index unmoved against the panel and the burner lit, the fault is not the approach pose. Suspect knob identification or rotation axis instead. The same applies if the policy, handed back from a merely cleared pose, reliably returns to the knob and turns it.
