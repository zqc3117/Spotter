**Why:**
Lowering the approach, targeting the visible potato, changing lateral alignment, alternating manual and policy closes, and reopening the gripper did not produce a verified lasting grasp. The logged repairs did not explicitly change the finger closing direction relative to the potato. This leaves grasp orientation as an untested alternative; the evidence does not establish that the bowl rim caused the misses.

**How to apply:**
For an elongated object in a dish, identify its long axis and the space available for each finger beside its body. Before repeating a position-only correction, test a wrist orientation that closes across the object's narrow dimension and lets both fingers descend inside the rim. Align above the object before descending, then let the policy perform the final close from the changed approach. If closing manually, verify that the object leaves the dish with the hand and remains held during the first policy movement.

**Falsify:**
This alternative is unsupported if clearly visible cross-body alignment with both fingers clear of the rim still produces empty closes or immediate slips. If successful and failed approaches already share that alignment, investigate target depth or grasp height instead of attributing the failure to orientation.
