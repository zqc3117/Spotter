**Why:** Reopening and handing back did not secure the fish. A later withdrawal carried the larger green vegetable while the fish remained on the shelf. Returning the distractor and changing the approach position also failed to establish a target grasp. These failures suggest testing target isolation and jaw alignment before another close; they do not establish a successful repair.

**How to apply:** After returning a wrong object, keep the gripper open and inspect the target and both finger paths. For an elongated target, try positioning the jaws across its body so closure brackets the target without including the neighboring object. Choose the approach from visible clearance rather than repeating the previous descent with a small position change. Once the target is visibly between the fingers at grasp depth, command the close and a short verification lift. Hand transport back only after observing the intended target move with the hand and the distractor remain behind.

**Falsify:** In a comparable cluttered shelf task, if clearly observed target isolation and cross-body jaw alignment still produce empty closes or distractor pickup, this alignment hypothesis is insufficient. If the intended target lifts but is then lost, investigate retention and transport instead of repeating alignment repairs.
