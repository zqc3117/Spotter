The policy closed on nothing over the pan five times in a row. One of my repair attempts finally caught the lime: I opened the fingers fully, came straight down, closed, and the aperture stopped at about 40 mm. I then lifted only about 2 cm and handed back to the policy for one chunk. During that chunk the policy commanded a full close and a fast sweep of about 7 cm. The aperture fell to about 1.6 mm, which means the lime was gone and was back in the pan. None of the later repairs caught it again. Some closed higher or lower, some came down with the fingers only partly open (which pushed the lime aside), and twice I set the hand above the lime and let the policy make the close. By then the lime had been knocked around the pan, so position estimates carried over from earlier attempts were already out of date.

**Why:** A policy stuck in a failed-grasp loop is still acting out its grasp phase. It does not know that the object is now held. Its next chunk is another close-and-yank, not a transport. A round object held in a two-finger pinch has almost no friction margin, so a hard squeeze or a jerk pops it out. If that happens low over a container, it rolls back to where it started. One successful catch was the most valuable state reached in the whole episode, and handing back straight away threw it away.

**How to apply:** When your own close on a rolling object stops well short of the empty-close aperture, treat that as the win and protect it. Lift well clear of the container rim with the gripper on hold. Check that the aperture has not changed after the lift. Then carry the object yourself until it is over, or close to, the destination, and only then hand back or place it yourself. Do not hand back at the source just because the policy's own plan starts with a grasp. Before each new attempt on a rolling object, find it again in the wrist view. After any miss, do not reuse coordinates from earlier attempts. Always descend with the fingers fully open, because fingers that are only partly open push it away.

**Falsify:** Next time, catch a rolling object with a repair, lift it clear and carry it toward the destination before handing back. If the policy still drops or re-grasps it within one or two chunks, the problem is handback itself and not handback at the source, and the right move is to finish the place yourself. If instead a handback right after a small lift at the source keeps the object held and the policy carries it away, this lesson is wrong.
