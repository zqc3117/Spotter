A hand that pulls back from a keypad with the display unchanged is usually re-approaching, not giving up.

**Why:**
These policies press by approaching, touching, backing off and approaching again. Interventions sent at the first withdrawal took over an arm that was turning back toward the button, and the corrections themselves overshot in front of the keypad.

**How to apply:**
- Let one withdraw-and-return pass; act when the withdrawal is followed by work elsewhere, or when the same cycle has repeated twice with nothing changed.
- Corrections in front of a keypad are a blow-up region: if you must act, change the approach height by a nudge, never with an absolute move.

**Falsify:**
A policy that after withdrawing never returns to the keypad on its own.
