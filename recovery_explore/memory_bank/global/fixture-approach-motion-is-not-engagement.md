**Why:**
A clearance move and return near a recorded missed contact restored substantial policy motion, but the fixture remained visibly active. The repair was ended because the hand had reached the control region and the controls became visible. The episode subsequently failed. This does not prove that further intervention would have succeeded, but it shows that restored arm motion was insufficient evidence of recovered fixture operation.

**How to apply:**
After a coarse reposition and short policy hand-back, evaluate the control rather than the distance travelled. Use the newly exposed view to check whether the fingers actually address the intended control and whether its state changes relative to the fixed panel. If the requested state is visibly unchanged, distinguish “approach improved” from “operation recovered.” When a specific remaining alignment error is visible, use that evidence for a small correction rather than treating proximity alone as a completed repair.

**Falsify:**
If repeated episodes show that the policy reliably completes fixture operation after such repositioning without further correction, then ending at the improved approach is sufficient in those conditions. Visible ongoing control movement also supports handing back without waiting for full task completion.
