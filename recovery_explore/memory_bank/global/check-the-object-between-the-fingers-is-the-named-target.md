The task named a small fish lying next to a much larger cucumber in a wall cabinet. The policy's
approach centred the wrist camera on the cucumber, and the fish only showed at the edge of the
frame, partly hidden behind one finger. Every repair I wrote aimed at the fish by name, but the
unprojected point and the check "object centred between the fingers" both kept landing on the
cucumber. I then spent attempts changing height, drift compensation, close timing, wrist yaw and
who makes the close. All of them shut on air around the wrong object, and the episode ran out of
policy steps.

**Why:** Up close, the wrist view is filled by whatever is biggest near the hand, so "something is
between the fingers" feels like confirmation of alignment. A pixel or unprojected point picked on
a cluttered shelf lands on the dominant surface. Handing back to the policy from a hover over the
distractor repeats the policy's own mistake, because that is the pose it chose. Tuning the grasp
mechanics cannot help when the aim is on the wrong object.

**How to apply:** Before closing, and before any hand-back, name what is between the fingers by its
appearance: colour, texture, shape, and features such as an eye or fins. Check that it matches the
instruction's noun in the wrist view and in one third-person view. If a different, larger object
fills the gap and the target sits at the frame edge or under a finger, re-aim first. Shift the
hover horizontally toward the target until the target, not the neighbour, sits centred between
the open fingers. Then descend from above. After two empty closes with an object visibly between
the fingers, check identity before you change height, timing or orientation.

**Falsify:** Next time a small target sits beside a larger distractor, centre the target by
appearance before descending. If the closes still shut on air, or the target still does not come
up on the lift, then wrong-object aim was not the cause. The fix then lies in grasp mechanics for
flat, low objects (closing height, finger orientation), not in target identity.
