**Why:** Both control and treatment failed. Reopening the fingers and returning to a lower approach before handing the close back to the policy did not secure the dispenser. A manual pinch also failed its lift test, and later Cartesian recovery moves missed. These observations establish failure of the attempted repairs, not the precise geometric cause. They motivate testing body-centered alignment rather than repeating height corrections near the missed grasp.

**How to apply:** For a pump dispenser with repeated empty closes, identify the broad bottle body separately from its narrow neck and pump. Try an approach that places the open fingers on opposite sides of that body, with clearance from the pump and counter. Verify that the body lies between the fingers before making a controlled close. Use a brief lift test and require the dispenser to move with the hand before handing transport back to the policy. If alignment cannot be reached reliably, do not treat another close as a completed repair.

**Falsify:** On another dispenser episode, visibly straddling the body followed by a controlled close still leaves the dispenser on the counter during the lift test. That would show this alignment-and-handoff alternative is insufficient and point toward contact, aperture, or reachability as the next issue to investigate.
